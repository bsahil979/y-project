**Status:** working literature notes, updated 28 September 2026. Full texts were checked for Markowitz, Ledoit-Wolf, Bailey-López de Prado, and the 1998 Sullivan-Timmermann-White discussion-paper version. The final Sullivan journal page was checked for its abstract and citation details. Publisher/abstract records only were accessible for Hamilton and Ang-Bekaert; verify required citation style before submission.

## Research question

Does conditioning covariance estimates on probabilities from a three-state Gaussian Hidden Markov Model improve later, unseen risk-adjusted results over a matched long-only minimum-variance portfolio, when the universe, observations, position cap, rebalancing dates, execution delay, and trading costs are held fixed?

The project combines established tools. The proposed contribution is a transparent, reproducible comparison in a narrowly defined sample, not a new HMM, a new portfolio optimizer, or a claim that regime switching is novel.

## What this means for the paper

The literature supports three narrow points: covariance matters to portfolio risk; Markov models provide a way to represent latent changes; and estimates and backtests can be misleading when noisy inputs or repeated strategy search are ignored. It does **not** establish that this project’s regime-conditioned allocation improves performance.

The defensible framing is a small empirical comparison of a fixed HMM-conditioned minimum-variance rule with a matched conventional minimum-variance rule. The HMM uses three Gaussian states and a 504-return rolling history; state covariances are shrunk by a fixed 0.5 toward unconditional covariance; both optimizers are long-only with a 40% per-stock cap. These design choices are implementation settings, not conclusions from the literature.

The exploratory 2020-2024 run is descriptive and favored the matched minimum-variance method on Sharpe. The later 2025 holdout produced a small positive HMM-minus-baseline Sharpe difference, but its paired bootstrap interval includes zero and 45 of 70 primary HMM fits reached the iteration cap without meeting tolerance. Neither result establishes an HMM advantage, statistical significance, investment advice, or a broad research gap.
